# Jurisdiction notes

These are outlines for plain language pages. They are not legal advice. Laws and rules change. Before launch, the owner should check the current official text and have a qualified person review the pages. Never write that the product "is compliant". Never invent timeframes, thresholds or officer details. Use `[CONFIRM: ...]` and `[TODO: ...]` markers.

Ask which jurisdictions apply. Do not assume. Include the sections for every jurisdiction the user selected.

## India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP)

Terms in the Act: the person is the Data Principal. The service is the Data Fiduciary.

Rights to explain in plain words, with how to use each one in the app:

1. **Access:** get a summary of the personal data processed. In the app: export.
2. **Correction and erasure:** correct or complete data, and ask for erasure. In the app: edit data on its page, and delete the account in Settings.
3. **Withdraw consent:** stop the processing the user agreed to. In the app: delete the account, or contact the grievance contact.
4. **Grievance redressal:** raise a complaint with the contact point. Show the Grievance Officer box.
5. **Nominate:** name another person to exercise these rights in case of death or incapacity. Explain how to request it, with `[CONFIRM: how nominations are handled]`.

Notice and consent: the signup checkbox must be affirmative and unticked by default.

Markers needed: grievance officer name, designation, email, response timeframe.

## EU and UK: GDPR and UK GDPR

Rights to explain: be informed, access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and rights about automated decisions. Also withdraw consent at any time, and complain to the supervisory authority.

Markers needed: controller identity and contact, legal basis for each purpose, data protection officer if required, international transfer mechanism, retention, and the supervisory authority.

If the app uses non essential cookies or analytics, a consent banner is likely needed. Mark it `[CONFIRM: cookie consent approach]`.

## United States: California (CCPA and CPRA) and other states

Rights that may apply: know, delete, correct, opt out of sale or sharing, limit use of sensitive data, and no discrimination for using rights.

Whether these laws apply depends on the business. Mark it `[CONFIRM: does this apply to us]`. Also mark how requests are submitted.

## Other or unknown

Use the common core: what data is collected, why, who processes it, how long it is kept, how to access, correct, export and delete it, and how to contact the owner.
